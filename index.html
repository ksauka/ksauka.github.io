I am a PhD Researcher and Research Scientist at the Hogeschool van Amsterdam, pursuing my doctorate at the University of Amsterdam. My research is in Human-Centred Explainable Conversational AI, with a focus on how AI systems should manage commitment under uncertainty to support appropriate human reliance.

The central problem my thesis addresses is premature commitment: a conversational system can answer before its interpretation of user intent is sufficiently resolved, and a human can accept an AI recommendation before independent evaluation has occurred. As part of a collaborative research programme, my doctoral work investigates the conditions under which conversational explanation delays, supports, or justifies commitment on both sides of this interaction.

My published work investigates how perceived explainability and anthropomorphic design jointly shape trust through social interpretation, and how a Dempster-Shafer belief state can serve as an interrogative gate — blocking answer generation when epistemic uncertainty is too high and prompting the system to seek clarification instead. A complementary behavioural study examines whether independent judgement and cognitive forcing before recommendation exposure improve correctness-sensitive reliance.

I am currently extending this work toward an integrated HC-XCAI reference architecture that combines AI-side interrogative gating, evidence-grounded retrieval, source provenance, and human-side commitment gates — with longer-term interest in interactive reasoning under uncertainty in agentic AI settings.
